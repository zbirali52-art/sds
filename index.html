Strong Determination Sitting (SDS)

The definitive guide to the most rigorous, plateau-shattering meditation practice known to modern mindfulness and ancient monasteries.

⚡ Ancient Ancestor of SDS

The Marathon Monks of Mount Hiei (Kaihōgyō)

Why did Buddha sit under the Bodhi tree vowing, "Let my skin, sinews, and bones dry up, but I shall not move until I attain enlightenment"? To understand the psychology of Adhiṭṭhāna, look at the most extreme spiritual athletes on Earth: The Tendai Buddhist Marathon Monks of Mount Hiei, Japan.

🗡️ The Lethal Vow (No Exit Route)

Over a 7-year trial called Kaihōgyō, the monk must walk and run 1,000 marathons through frozen mountain peaks in thin straw sandals. But their defining rule is psychological: the monk must carry a sheathed knife (kaiken) and a hemp rope at all times. If on any single day they fail to complete the distance or give up, ancient tradition dictates they must take their own life on the mountain. There is zero retreat.

🧘 The 9-Day Stillness Crucible (Dō-iri)

In the final stage, the monk enters the temple for Dō-iri: 9 continuous days with zero food, zero water, zero sleep, and no lying down. Flanked by two attendants to ensure posture never collapses, they chant non-stop. By day 5, bodily tissue undergoes radical purification. By day 9, monks report that they can hear ashes falling in distant incense burners and smell rain miles away.

💡 The Core Psychological Law: Eliminate The Escape Hatch

When you enter an Adhiṭṭhāna sit, your mind begs to move because it believes "moving is an option". The moment you make non-movement 100% absolute—just like the Marathon Monks carrying their rope—the ego realizes its whining and bargaining are completely futile. When resistance dies, pain turns into raw, harmless energy.

Suffering = Pain × Resistance
Pain is raw physical sensation. Suffering is the mind fighting it.
Drop your mental resistance to zero (Resistance = 0) → Suffering vanishes to Zero.

You are free to meditate with eyes Open or Closed. If open, maintain a soft, unfocused gaze pointing downward at the floor 3–4 feet ahead (Zen style). If closed, seal them gently (Vipassana style). Once chosen, do not wander your gaze, glance at the clock, or look around.

⏳ The 4 Phases of an SDS Sit
Phase 1 (0 – 25 Min)
The Honeycomb
Pleasant settling. Gentle respiration. The mind is cooperative and feels peaceful.
Phase 2 (25 – 40 Min)
The First Fires
Dull muscular burn in lower back or ankles. The ego whispers subtle excuses: "Just stretch once." Do not negotiate.
Phase 3 (40 – 55 Min)
The Crucible (Ego Tantrum)
Peak physical fire. The ego unleashes panic, catastrophizing, and bargaining. Surrender resistance entirely.
Phase 4 (55 – 60 Min)
Transcendence
Pain detaches from identity. You become the vast, silent space in which sensation arises and passes away.
🪑 Recommended Sitting Postures

Burmese Style

Both legs resting on the floor in front of each other, ankles uncrossed. Easiest on knees and hips for 60m sits.

Half / Full Lotus

One or both feet resting on opposite thighs. Exceptional triangular stability; requires open hips.

Seiza (Kneeling Bench)

Kneeling with hips resting on a zafu or angled meditation bench. Keeps spine upright with minimum knee torsion.

Straight-Backed Chair

Feet flat on the floor, spine away from the backrest, head balanced. A valid and effective option.
